Lectures 1–2 · Misha Boroditsky

Systematic fixed income

The central problem is comparison. Bonds differ in maturity, coupon, optionality, liquidity, and credit. We transform price into risk-controlled curve coordinates so unlike instruments can be compared on common terms.

Cash flows→Curves→Spreads→Residuals→Portfolio
01Market structureWhy fixed income is not equity with couponsFive layers

Observation

Corporate fixed income has far more instruments than listed equities, but dramatically fewer trades per instrument. A single issuer can have dozens of bonds with different coupons, maturities, covenants, and seniority. Many bonds are held by pensions and insurers and may not trade for days.

Intuition

Liquidity is fragmented. There may be no continuously visible order book for the exact bond you want. The common workflow is request-for-quote: the buy side asks several dealers, dealers price inventory and risk, and the client chooses or declines. A theoretical fair value is therefore not enough; the system also needs a probability of execution and an estimate of trading cost.

Mathematics

A simple implementation objective is expected value after cost:

Expected edge = model alpha − expected spread − market impact − financing cost

Dealer count creates a trade-off: more dealers improve price discovery but can leak information and reduce response quality. Optimize the expected all-in result, not merely the displayed quote.

Trading / modeling consequence

A usable system joins reference data, evaluated prices, dealer quotes, TRACE prints, liquidity features, inventory, and risk. “Time is alpha”: a slightly imperfect executable price can dominate a perfect estimate delivered after the opportunity disappears.

Common confusion Electronic does not mean exchange-like. Many electronic credit trades still use RFQ protocols.

Remember In illiquid markets, the execution model is part of the alpha model.

02Bond mathematicsFrom dated cash flows to price, yield, duration, and DV01Core exam material

Observation

A bond is a schedule of promised cash flows. Price and yield move in opposite directions; long-maturity, low-coupon bonds react more to a rate move than short-maturity, high-coupon bonds.

Intuition

Yield is a compressed quote, not a set of cash flows. It is the single discount rate that reproduces the observed price. Duration asks, “How far into the future is my money effectively received?” DV01 converts that percentage sensitivity into the dollar amount traders can hedge.

Mathematics

Zero couponP = F e−yT
Coupon bondP = Σ CFk e−ytk
Duration approximationΔP / P ≈ −D · Δy
Dollar riskDV01 = −10−4 ∂P/∂y ≈ D·P·10−4

Example: a $100 bond with duration 8 loses approximately $0.08 when yield rises 10 bp: −8 × 100 × 0.001 = −0.80. For a 1 bp move, DV01 is about $0.08.

Trading / modeling consequence

Compare positions and build hedges in DV01, not face value. Equal notionals can have very different rate risk. Use convexity when rate moves are large or when comparing bonds with materially different cash-flow profiles.

Common confusion Yield is not the same as the zero rate used for every payment date.

Settlement Dirty price = clean price + accrued interest. The dirty price is the economic value exchanged.

03Reference curvesBootstrap the Treasury zero curve before measuring creditRates foundation

Observation

Treasuries are coupon instruments, yet valuation requires a discount factor for each future payment date. A quoted Treasury yield is therefore not itself the complete discount curve.

Intuition

Bootstrapping solves maturities in order. Short instruments reveal early discount factors; those known factors are used to strip coupons from the next bond, revealing the next unknown point. The curve becomes a common risk-free coordinate system.

Mathematics

Discount factord(0,T)=e−z(T)T
Cash-flow valueP=Σ CFkd(0,tk)
Forward ratef(t₁,t₂)=[z₂t₂−z₁t₁]/(t₂−t₁)

The forward rate is the future borrowing rate implied today by two discount factors. It is an arbitrage-consistency relationship, not a guaranteed forecast.

Trading / modeling consequence

Interpolate discount factors or a smooth parameterization, not raw coupon yields. Once the Treasury curve is stable, a corporate bond can be decomposed into rate value plus credit/liquidity spread.

Graph reading Level is the broad rate environment; slope compares short and long rates; curvature captures a middle-maturity hump or trough.

Remember First explain the risk-free curve, then explain the residual.

04Expected returnSeparate carry, pull-to-par, and rolldownScenario logic

Observation

A bond can earn or lose money even when the market experiences no parallel rate shift. Coupon income, convergence toward par, and movement along the curve all affect holding-period return.

Intuition

Carry holds the bond's own yield constant while time passes. Rolldown holds the curve fixed while the bond becomes a shorter-maturity instrument. These are different counterfactuals and should not be mixed.

Mathematics

Carrycoupon income + pull-to-par
Rolldown approximationRoll P&L ≈ −D·P·Δycurve
Total scenarioReturn ≈ carry + roll + spread move + rate move

On an upward-sloping curve, aging from a higher-yield point to a lower-yield point creates a price gain, all else equal. An inverted curve can reverse the sign.

Trading / modeling consequence

Report expected return by component. A strategy that appears profitable from carry may actually be a disguised curve bet. Stress alternative curve shapes rather than assuming rolldown is free alpha.

Common confusion “Rates unchanged” can mean unchanged bond yield or unchanged curve. Those assumptions produce different P&L.

Exam check State explicitly what is held fixed.

05Credit coordinatesConvert price into a comparable spreadRelative-value input

Observation

Two corporate bonds with different coupons and maturities cannot be compared reliably by price or yield alone. Their Treasury exposure and cash-flow timing differ.

Intuition

A spread is the compensation above a reference curve for credit, liquidity, optionality, and technical effects. The z-spread asks for one constant shift applied to every zero-curve point so that discounted promised cash flows match the market price.

Mathematics

P = Σ CFk · exp{−[z(tk) + s]tk}

G-spread compares yield with an interpolated government yield. I-spread uses the swap curve. Z-spread uses the full zero curve. OAS additionally removes the value of embedded options through a rate model.

Trading / modeling consequence

Use z-spread for option-free bonds and OAS when callability matters. A wide spread is not automatically cheap: it may reflect higher default probability, lower recovery, poor liquidity, or an embedded option.

Common confusion A spread is a coordinate, not a complete expected-return forecast.

Credit loss lens Roughly, expected loss ≈ probability of default × loss given default.

06Issuer curvesNelson–Siegel turns scattered bonds into level, slope, and curvatureModel core

Observation

Bonds from one issuer form a noisy cross-section. The common component is the issuer spread curve; security-specific pricing differences appear as residuals around it.

Intuition

Nelson–Siegel is parsimonious: one parameter controls the long-run level, one the short-end slope, one the medium-term hump, and one the speed of decay. The model smooths noise without demanding a separate parameter for every maturity.

Mathematics

Forward spreadf(t)=L+Se−Dt+CDte−Dt
Zero spreadz(t)=L+(S+C)(1−e−Dt)/(Dt)−Ce−Dt
Boundary behaviorz(0)=L+S; z(∞)=L
Residualεi=si−ŝ(ti)

Fit in spread space, not raw price space. Weight observations by reliability—often liquidity, spread duration, or inverse variance—so a stale or tiny trade does not dominate the curve.

Trading / modeling consequence

A positive residual means the bond trades wider than the fitted curve: potentially cheap. A negative residual means rich. Require enough bonds and inspect residual stability; four parameters fitted to four bonds explains everything and predicts nothing.

Graph reading The curve is fair value conditional on the chosen peer group. The vertical distance from a bond to the curve is the raw signal.

Remember Long cheap / short rich is only valid after risk and liquidity controls.

07From signal to portfolioNeutralize the risks you do not intend to tradeImplementation

Observation

A long-cheap/short-rich pair can still lose money because it is long duration, short credit beta, concentrated in one issuer, or impossible to execute at modeled prices.

Intuition

Alpha is a forecast; a portfolio is a constrained expression of forecasts. Neutralization isolates the intended residual while optimization balances expected edge against covariance risk and cost.

Mathematics

Duration hedgewLDV01L + wSDV01S = 0
UtilityU = αᵀw − λwᵀLᵀCLw − costs(w)
Neutrality examplesBᵀw = 0

B can contain Treasury level/slope/curvature, sector, rating, issuer, spread duration, and liquidity exposures. The optimizer should see the same costs and constraints the trader will face.

Trading / modeling consequence

Backtest signal, execution, and risk together. Portfolio trading and ETFs can reduce execution friction but may inject noisy individual-bond marks. Capacity and turnover determine whether a residual is monetizable.

Common confusion Dollar neutrality is not duration neutrality.

Final test Can you explain the expected return, dominant risk, hedge, cost, and failure mode before placing the trade?

Lecture 3 · Dmitry Rakhlin

Quantitative equity framework

The central problem is identification. An observed return pattern may be compensation for risk, behavioral mispricing, a statistical accident, or an untradeable artifact. The research process must separate these possibilities.

Hypothesis→Exposure→Test→Portfolio→Attribution
08DefinitionsFactor exposure is not the same thing as alphaFoundation

Observation

Many managers earn returns that disappear after controlling for market, size, value, profitability, investment, momentum, or industry exposures.

Intuition

Beta is systematic co-movement. A factor premium is compensation associated with that exposure. Pure alpha is the remaining expected return that cannot be reproduced cheaply with known factors. Once an “alpha” becomes known and crowded, its return may decay or migrate into a recognized factor.

Mathematics

Ri,t−Rf,t = αi + βiᵀFt + εi,t

The intercept is conditional on the factors included. Add a missing factor and apparent alpha may vanish.

Trading / modeling consequence

Every research report should show gross return, known-factor exposures, residual alpha, turnover, cost, capacity, and decay. Investors should know whether they are paying active fees for a cheap beta.

Common confusion “Alpha factor” is industry language for a predictor; it does not prove regression alpha.

Remember Alpha is model-relative.

09IdentificationFama–MacBeth: exposure first, price of exposure secondTwo-stage method

Observation

A factor can explain an asset through time without earning a positive premium across assets. Conversely, a cross-sectional relation is hard to interpret unless exposures are measured first.

Intuition

Stage 1 asks what each asset is exposed to. Stage 2 asks whether the market rewards that exposure. Beta and lambda answer different questions and use different dimensions of the data.

Mathematics

Stage 1 · for each asset iRi,t−Rf,t=αi+βiᵀFt+εi,t
Stage 2 · for each date tRi,t=λ0,t+β̂iᵀλt+ui,t

Average the time series of λt estimates and use their time variation for standard errors. Estimated betas introduce measurement error, so implementation details matter.

Trading / modeling consequence

A significant beta says the asset moves with a factor. A significant average lambda says the exposure is priced. Neither alone proves a standalone trading strategy survives costs.

Common confusion Stage 2 is across many assets at one date, not another time-series regression.

Memory sentence Beta is quantity of risk; lambda is price per unit of risk.

10Factor constructionFama–French portfolios isolate common return dimensionsPortfolio mechanics

Observation

CAPM leaves systematic patterns: small firms and high book-to-market firms historically earn different average returns. Profitability and investment add further dimensions.

Intuition

Factor returns are built from many stocks so idiosyncratic noise diversifies away. Long-short comparisons attempt to change one characteristic while holding others approximately balanced.

Mathematics

MarketMKT = Rm−Rf
SizeSMB = small − big
ValueHML = high B/M − low B/M
Profitability / investmentRMW, CMA

A 2×3 sort creates six portfolios for size and book-to-market. A full 3×3×3×3 independent sort across four characteristics creates 81 cells: better isolation, but fewer stocks per cell and noisier portfolios.

Trading / modeling consequence

When comparing cells, move along one dimension while holding the others fixed. Verify that the constructed factor is diversified, investable, and not dominated by microcaps.

Common confusion A factor is a portfolio return, not a company attribute. Book-to-market is a characteristic; HML is the long-short factor built from it.

Diagnostic Check correlations among factors: overlapping definitions can double-count the same economic exposure.

11Statistical reliabilityThe factor zoo makes t = 2 an unsafe finish lineResearch discipline

Observation

If researchers try hundreds of signals, some will look significant by chance. A story can be invented after the pattern is found, making noise appear economically meaningful.

Intuition

One hypothesis at 5% significance tolerates a 5% false-positive rate. Hundreds of correlated trials create a selection problem. The published winner is conditioned on having won.

Mathematics

Test statistict = estimate / standard error
Type I errorReject H₀ when H₀ is true
Type II errorFail to reject H₀ when H₀ is false
GRS nullH₀: α₁=…=αN=0

Use a higher hurdle, multiple-testing adjustments, out-of-sample data, and tests that were not selected after seeing the return. The GRS test asks whether all portfolio alphas are jointly zero under the factor model.

Trading / modeling consequence

Require robustness across periods, countries, definitions, weighting schemes, and known-factor controls. Separate discovery, validation, and live monitoring datasets.

Coin-toss intuition Finding one person with 30 heads after searching a billion people does not imply skill.

Remember Replication should preserve the hypothesis, not re-optimize until it works.

12Economic explanationBehavioral bias can create a pattern; limits to arbitrage can preserve itWhy anomalies persist

Observation

Investors underreact, overreact, anchor on salient numbers, seek confirming evidence, extrapolate recent trends, and become overconfident. Mispricing can persist even when sophisticated investors recognize it.

Intuition

A bias explains who makes the mistake. Limits to arbitrage explain why informed capital does not instantly remove it: short-sale constraints, funding risk, benchmark pressure, career risk, no close substitute, and uncertain convergence time.

Mathematics

Net opportunity = mispricing × convergence probability − cost − funding risk − crowding loss

The return may be strongest where trading is hardest. That creates the classic tension between statistical magnitude and realizable capacity.

Trading / modeling consequence

Validate the proposed mechanism directly. If the story is underreaction to information, test forecast revisions or post-event drift—not only the final long-short return.

Common confusion A plausible behavioral story is supporting evidence, not proof.

Research question Who is constrained, who supplies liquidity, and what causes convergence?

13Fundamental valuationConnect dividends, growth, ROE, and price-to-bookValue with structure

Observation

A low price-to-book stock is not necessarily cheap. Low valuation may be justified by weak profitability, excessive investment, or a high required return.

Intuition

Value comes from future cash distributions. Growth creates value only when retained capital earns an adequate return. The P/B–ROE relation explains why value and quality cannot be analyzed independently.

Mathematics

Dividend discount modelV=Σ Dt/(1+r)t
Gordon growthV=D₁/(r−g), r>g
Sustainable growthg=ROE·(1−payout)
Price-to-bookP/B=(ROE−g)/(r−g)

If ROE exceeds the required return, reinvestment can create value. If ROE is below the required return, growth can destroy value.

Trading / modeling consequence

Compare valuation within a framework that controls for profitability, growth, leverage, and risk. “Long the lower P/B stock” is not a complete investment thesis.

Common confusion Growth raises value only when the incremental return on retained capital justifies it.

Memory chain DDM → Gordon → retention × ROE → P/B.

14Research-to-productionA signal is only one component of an investment processSystem architecture

Observation

Strong backtest returns often weaken after risk controls, portfolio constraints, turnover limits, implementation shortfall, and live data delays.

Intuition

The alpha model forecasts returns; the risk model describes co-movement; the optimizer converts forecasts into holdings; implementation converts holdings into trades; attribution tests whether realized returns came from the intended source.

Mathematics

maxw αᵀw − λwᵀΣw − costs(Δw), subject to exposures, leverage, liquidity, and turnover constraints

Ex-post attribution compares realized return with ex-ante factor exposures. Persistent unexplained slippage can reveal a broken cost model or stale signal.

Trading / modeling consequence

Monitor forecast correlation, breadth, turnover, capacity, crowding, and decay. A lower-gross-alpha model with stable data and low implementation cost can be superior to a fragile high-t-stat backtest.

Final checklist Economic rationale, clean data, robust test, known-factor horse race, realistic costs, capacity, decay, and live attribution.

Remember The portfolio is the product; the signal is an input.

One-page mental map

From raw observations to a defensible trade

1

Normalize

Convert bond prices to curve-relative spreads; convert stock characteristics to diversified factor portfolios.

2

Explain

Separate common risk from security-specific residual. State the economic mechanism before testing returns.

3

Test

Use out-of-sample evidence, known-factor controls, robust standard errors, and multiple-testing discipline.

4

Construct

Neutralize unintended exposures. Optimize expected return against covariance, liquidity, turnover, and cost.

5

Attribute

Compare realized P&L with the original forecast, risk exposures, execution assumptions, and decay model.

Questions before trading

  • What is the raw observation?
  • What risk explains it?
  • What remains after controls?
  • Why should it persist?
  • Can it be executed after cost?
  • What result would falsify it?
