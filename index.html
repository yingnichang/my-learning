I

Lectures 1–2

Systematic fixed income

1

Bond cash flows

A zero-coupon bond has one payment. A coupon bond is a portfolio of dated cash flows. Price is their present value.

2

Yield and price

Yield is the constant discount rate implied by price. Higher yield means lower price because future cash flows are discounted more heavily.

3

Duration and DV01

Duration gives percentage sensitivity; DV01 gives dollar sensitivity. Use DV01 to size hedges and compare positions.

4

Zero curve

Bootstrap zero rates from traded Treasury instruments so each cash flow is discounted at its own maturity-specific rate.

5

Carry and rolldown

Carry assumes the bond's yield stays fixed. Rolldown holds the curve fixed while the bond ages to a shorter maturity.

6

Credit relative value

Compare bonds after controlling for rates and cash-flow timing. Z-spread is the constant shift to the zero curve that matches price.

II

Lecture 3

Factors, alpha, and empirical tests

1

Exposure versus reward

Beta is how much an asset loads on a factor. Lambda is the market's expected reward per unit of that exposure. Alpha is the unexplained remainder.

2

Time series first

For each asset, regress returns through time against factor returns. The estimated coefficients are that asset's known exposures for stage two.

3

Cross-section second

At a fixed time, compare returns across many assets using their estimated betas. This asks whether greater exposure earned greater return.

4

2 × 3 portfolios

Sort on size and book-to-market. Compare horizontally or vertically only when the other sorting dimension is being held fixed.

5

False discoveries

A Type I error calls noise alpha. A Type II error misses real alpha. Data snooping raises false-positive risk across many attempted factors.

6

Tradability

A statistically real anomaly still needs capacity after transaction costs, short-sale constraints, crowding, and signal decay.
