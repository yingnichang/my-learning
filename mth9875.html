The Volatility Surface

The first three lectures build one continuous argument: empirical market data reject constant-volatility thinking; stochastic and rough volatility explain changing variance and short-time scaling; local volatility matches the vanilla surface; affine forward-variance models then unify classical and rough Heston and make characteristic-function pricing tractable.

01Affine modelsHow Lecture 3 fits the course — and what we are ultimately trying to computep. 1

Observation

Lecture 3 rewrites classical and rough Heston in one forward-variance language and then exploits affine structure to compute transforms.

Intuition

The goal is not to predict one future stock price. For an option maturing at \(T\), we need the whole risk-neutral distribution of possible \(S_T\) values. A call pays \((S_T-K)^+\), so two models can have the same \(E_t[S_T]\) but very different option values if one distribution is much more dispersed.

The lecture therefore follows a chain: model variance dynamics → encode the distribution of \(S_T\) with a transform → solve for the transform → recover option prices. The Riccati equation is the engine in the middle, not the final objective.

Mathematics / roadmap

\[ V\text{ dynamics}\;\longrightarrow\;\xi_t(u)\;\longrightarrow\;\text{affine CGF}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Riccati}\;\longrightarrow\;\phi_T\;\longrightarrow\;\text{option prices}. \]

Here \(X_t=\log S_t\), \(\xi_t(u)=E_t[V_u]\), and \(\phi\) denotes the characteristic function of the terminal log-price or log-return.

Trading / modeling consequence

Characteristic functions allow European option prices across many strikes to be computed efficiently without solving a new high-dimensional pricing PDE for every strike.

Common confusionWe are not trying to estimate a single \(S_T\). We are trying to characterize the distribution of \(S_T\), because option value depends on the shape of that distribution.
Memory ruleDynamics → distribution → option price; Riccati connects dynamics to the distribution.
02Power-law algebraHow exponents transformWorked rules

Intuition

The fattest tail dominates sums and maxima. Transformations such as (Y=X^\alpha) rescale the exponent.

Mathematics

\[\zeta_{X_1+\cdots+X_n}=\min_i\zeta_i,\qquad \zeta_{\max(X_i)}=\min_i\zeta_i.\]
\[Y=X^\alpha\quad\Rightarrow\quad \zeta_Y=\frac{\zeta_X}{\alpha}.\]
Worked derivation of (Y=X^\alpha)
  1. (P(Y>y)=P(X^\alpha>y)).
  2. For (alpha>0), this is (P(X>y^{1/\alpha})).
  3. Use the tail law: (P(X>x)\sim x^{-\zeta_X}).
  4. Then (P(Y>y)\sim (y^{1/\alpha})^{-\zeta_X}=y^{-\zeta_X/\alpha}).
  5. Therefore (zeta_Y=\zeta_X/\alpha).

Modeling consequence

This algebra lets one connect power laws across trade size, market impact, firm size, and returns without refitting every relation from scratch.

03Forward varianceDeriving the Heston forward-variance ODEp. 2

Observation

Taking a conditional expectation of the Heston variance SDE removes the future Brownian shock and leaves a deterministic ODE in the future-maturity variable \(u\).

Intuition

The Brownian increment has conditional mean zero. Mean reversion survives because it is the predictable part of future variance dynamics. The ODE says: if today's variance is away from its long-run level, our expected future variance gradually moves back toward that level.

State versus parameters: to forecast future variance we still need the fixed model parameters \(\bar V,\lambda,\eta\). Saying Heston is Markov in \(V_t\) does not mean those parameters are unnecessary; it means that, once the parameters are fixed, we do not also need the whole past path \(V_{t-1},V_{t-2},\ldots\).

Mathematics

\[ dV_u=-\lambda(V_u-\bar V)\,du+\eta\sqrt{V_u}\,dW_u, \qquad \xi_t(u)=E_t[V_u]. \]
Worked steps
  1. Fix today's information time \(t\) and let \(u\) move into the future.
  2. Take \(E_t[\cdot]\) on both sides of the variance SDE.
  3. Use \(E_t[V_u]=\xi_t(u)\).
  4. The stochastic increment has conditional mean zero: \(E_t[\sqrt{V_u}\,dW_u]=0\).
  5. Therefore \(d\xi_t(u)=-\lambda[\xi_t(u)-\bar V]du\).
  6. Divide by \(du\): \(\partial_u\xi_t(u)=-\lambda[\xi_t(u)-\bar V]\).

Trading / modeling consequence

The forward curve approaches the long-run variance as the maturity \(u\) moves away from today.

Common confusionThis ODE evolves in \(u\), the future maturity. It is different from the stochastic \(t\)-dynamics of the forward curve.
Memory ruleParameters tell the rules; \(V_t\) is the current state; history is unnecessary in classical Heston.
04ODE solutionSolving the forward-variance ODE step by stepp. 2

Observation

The Heston forward variance curve decays exponentially from current variance toward the long-run mean.

Intuition

First measure the distance from equilibrium, \(V-\bar V\). Heston says that distance shrinks at a constant proportional rate \(\lambda\), exactly like exponential cooling or decay. If \(V_t>\bar V\), the expected curve slopes down; if \(V_t<\bar V\), it slopes up.

Mathematics

\[ \xi_t(u)=\bar V+(V_t-\bar V)e^{-\lambda(u-t)}. \]
Worked steps
  1. Start from \(\partial_u\xi_t(u)=-\lambda[\xi_t(u)-\bar V]\).
  2. Define \(y(u)=\xi_t(u)-\bar V\).
  3. Then \(y'(u)=-\lambda y(u)\).
  4. Separate variables: \(dy/y=-\lambda\,du\).
  5. Integrate from \(t\) to \(u\): \(\log y(u)-\log y(t)=-\lambda(u-t)\).
  6. Exponentiate: \(y(u)=y(t)e^{-\lambda(u-t)}\).
  7. Use \(\xi_t(t)=V_t\), so \(y(t)=V_t-\bar V\).
  8. Add \(\bar V\) back.

Trading / modeling consequence

The very rigid exponential shape is one reason classical Heston cannot reproduce every empirically observed variance-memory pattern.

Common confusion\(\bar V\) is a fixed long-run model parameter here, not another random state variable.
Memory ruleSubtract the mean → exponential decay → add the mean back.
05MartingaleWhy a conditional expectation is a martingalep. 2

Observation

For each fixed future date \(u\), \(\xi_t(u)=E[V_u\mid\mathcal F_t]\) changes as new information arrives, but its change has no predictable direction.

Intuition

Think of \(V_u\) as one fixed future outcome that we keep re-forecasting. At 9am your best estimate might be 4%. At noon it may become 5% or 3% after new information. Before the noon information arrives, however, your best expectation of the noon forecast is still the 9am forecast. Otherwise you should already revise the 9am forecast now.

So a martingale does not mean the forecast stays constant. It means forecast revisions are unpredictable with the information currently available.

Mathematics

\[ \xi_t(u)=E[V_u\mid\mathcal F_t]. \]
Tower-property proof
  1. Fix the future maturity \(u\).
  2. Take two information times \(s
  3. Start from \(\xi_t(u)=E[V_u\mid\mathcal F_t]\).
  4. At time \(s\), ask for the expected later forecast: \(E[\xi_t(u)\mid\mathcal F_s]\).
  5. Apply the tower property: \(E[E[V_u\mid\mathcal F_t]\mid\mathcal F_s]=E[V_u\mid\mathcal F_s]\).
  6. The right side is \(\xi_s(u)\).
  7. Thus \(E_s[\xi_t(u)]=\xi_s(u)\), the martingale property.

Trading / modeling consequence

Because \(\xi_t(u)\) is a martingale in information time \(t\), its SDE has no predictable \(dt\) drift under the pricing measure. This lets us focus on the diffusion term when deriving forward-variance dynamics.

Common confusionMartingale in \(t\) does not mean flat as a function of maturity \(u\). The forward curve can slope in \(u\) while each fixed-\(u\) forecast is a martingale in \(t\).
Memory ruleToday's best forecast of tomorrow's best forecast equals today's best forecast.
06KernelsWhat a kernel means generallypp. 2–5

Observation

A kernel tells us how strongly a volatility shock at time \(t\) changes forward variance at a future maturity \(u\).

Intuition

Think of the kernel as a memory weighting function. A shock happens today. The kernel answers: how much of that shock is still felt one day, one week, one month, or one year from now?

An exponential kernel has one characteristic decay speed. A power-law kernel has no single decay speed; it blends many timescales and therefore carries richer memory.

Mathematics

\[ d\xi_t(u)=\kappa(u-t)\sqrt{V_t}\,dW_t. \]

The lag is \(\tau=u-t\). Classical Heston uses \(\kappa(\tau)=\eta e^{-\lambda\tau}\).

Trading / modeling consequence

The shape of \(\kappa\) controls memory, roughness, how many state factors are needed, and the form of the transform equation.

Common confusionA kernel is not a probability density here; it is a response weight.
Memory ruleKernel = how today's shock echoes across future horizons.
07Classical HestonThe exponential kernel and why one state is enoughp. 2

Observation

The Heston forward curve reacts most strongly at short maturities, and the shock loading decays exponentially with future lag.

Intuition

An exponential has a special “forgetting” property: once you know the current value of the exponentially weighted memory, you do not need to reconstruct the whole past. That is why classical Heston can compress volatility history into one current variance state \(V_t\) (given fixed parameters).

Mathematics

\[ \kappa_H(\tau)=\eta e^{-\lambda\tau}, \qquad d\xi_t(u)=\kappa_H(u-t)\sqrt{V_t}\,dW_t. \]
Worked steps
  1. Start from \(\xi_t(u)=\bar V+(V_t-\bar V)e^{-\lambda(u-t)}\).
  2. For a fixed maturity \(u\), new randomness enters through \(V_t\).
  3. The loading of a change in \(V_t\) is \(e^{-\lambda(u-t)}\).
  4. The diffusion of \(V_t\) is \(\eta\sqrt{V_t}dW_t\).
  5. Multiply the loading by that diffusion to obtain \(\eta e^{-\lambda(u-t)}\sqrt{V_t}dW_t\).

Trading / modeling consequence

One exponential decay scale \(\lambda\) corresponds to a finite-dimensional Markov representation with one volatility factor.

Common confusionYou still need the fixed parameters \(\bar V,\lambda,\eta\). “One state” means one evolving volatility state, not one total input.
Memory ruleOne exponential timescale → one memory state.
08Rough HestonThe power-law kernel: memory with many timescalespp. 2–3

Observation

Rough Heston replaces exponential short-memory behavior with a singular power-law response governed by \(H\).

Intuition

With \(H<1/2\), the exponent \(H-1/2\) is negative. The kernel is therefore very large at tiny lags: very recent shocks matter intensely. But unlike one exponential, a power law does not have one characteristic decay speed. Its effect spans many horizons, which is why rough volatility behaves like a model with many memory factors.

Mathematics

\[ \alpha=H+\frac12,\qquad \kappa_0(\tau)=\frac{\nu}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\tau^{\alpha-1} =\frac{\nu}{\Gamma(H+1/2)}\tau^{H-1/2}. \]

For \(\lambda=0\), \(d\xi_t(u)=\kappa_0(u-t)\sqrt{V_t}\,dW_t\).

Trading / modeling consequence

The power-law kernel links the empirical short-scale roughness from Lecture 2 directly to a tractable pricing model.

Common confusionThe singularity at \(\tau\downarrow0\) does not mean the model is automatically ill-defined; the relevant integrated quantities can remain finite.
Memory ruleRough kernel = very strong recent response + no single memory timescale.
09Non-MarkovianityWhy rough Heston cannot be summarized by \(V_t\) alonepp. 3–5

Observation

In rough Heston, two histories can arrive at the same current variance \(V_t\) yet imply different future variance behavior.

Intuition

Imagine two markets both have \(V_t=4\%\). In one, variance has just fallen from a long high-volatility episode; in the other, variance has just jumped from a quiet period. A rough power-law memory can distinguish these histories even though the current number \(V_t\) is identical.

The missing information is summarized by the whole forward-variance curve \(\{\xi_t(u):u>t\}\). Once that whole curve is known, we no longer need the raw historical path. Thus rough Heston is non-Markovian in the one-dimensional state \(V_t\), but Markovian in an infinite-dimensional curve-valued state.

Mathematics

\[ E[V_u\mid\mathcal F_t]\neq E[V_u\mid V_t]\quad\text{in general}, \qquad \text{state}=\{\xi_t(u):u>t\}. \]

Trading / modeling consequence

The state needed for exact rough dynamics is a function rather than one scalar, which explains both the richer memory and the numerical challenge.

Common confusionFixed parameters such as \(H,\nu,\lambda,\rho\) are still needed. The Markov question asks what evolving state summarizes history once those parameters are specified.
Memory ruleClassical Heston: current variance summarizes history. Rough Heston: the whole forward curve summarizes history.
10ApproximationWhy a rough kernel behaves like infinitely many exponential factorspp. 4–5

Observation

The rough power-law kernel can be represented as a continuous mixture of exponential kernels, and then approximated by a finite sum of exponentials.

Intuition

Each exponential \(e^{-x\tau}\) behaves like one Heston-style memory factor with mean-reversion speed \(x\). A power law mixes a continuum of speeds: very fast factors, medium factors, slow factors, and everything in between. That is why exact rough volatility is naturally infinite-dimensional.

The Gamma function helps derive the representation, but the reason we need many factors is not that Gamma generalizes factorials. The many factors come from the fact that the power law equals an integral over a continuum of exponential decay rates.

Mathematics and Gamma derivation

\[ \Gamma(1-\alpha)=\int_0^\infty y^{-\alpha}e^{-y}\,dy. \]
From Gamma to a mixture of exponentials
  1. Set \(y=x\tau\), so \(dy=\tau\,dx\).
  2. Then \(\Gamma(1-\alpha)=\tau^{1-\alpha}\int_0^\infty x^{-\alpha}e^{-x\tau}dx\).
  3. Rearrange: \[ \tau^{\alpha-1}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_0^\infty x^{-\alpha}e^{-x\tau}dx. \]
  4. Therefore \[ \kappa_0(\tau)=\frac{\nu}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)} \int_0^\infty x^{-\alpha}e^{-x\tau}dx. \]
  5. Numerically discretize the integral: \[ \kappa_0(\tau)\approx\sum_{i=1}^N w_i e^{-\lambda_i\tau}. \]
  6. Each exponential term becomes one Markov memory factor.

Trading / modeling consequence

A finite multifactor model can approximate rough volatility while remaining Markovian in a larger finite-dimensional state, at the cost of more computation and parameters.

Common confusion\(\Gamma(n)=(n-1)!\) explains fractional-order calculus, but it is the continuum of exponential decay rates—not factorial growth—that creates the many-factor interpretation.
Memory rulePower law = continuum of exponentials = continuum of memory timescales.
11Affine definitionWhat “affine” means in this coursep. 5

Observation

An affine model has a log transform that depends on its state through a linear term plus a constant.

Intuition

“Affine” does not say the actual volatility path is a straight line. It says that after we move to a transform of the future distribution, the dependence on today's state becomes simple. This is like replacing a difficult nonlinear object with a coordinate system in which the state enters as \(A+B\times\text{state}\).

Mathematics

\[ \log E[e^{zX_T}\mid X_t=x]=A(T-t,z)+B(T-t,z)x. \]

Trading / modeling consequence

This simple state dependence is what allows option-pricing transforms to be reduced to deterministic Riccati equations instead of solving the full stochastic system path by path.

Common confusionAffine describes the transform's dependence on the state, not the shape of the sample path.
Memory ruleAffine = complicated dynamics, simple transform dependence.
12Affine CGFWhat the affine-CGF formula is really sayingp. 6

Observation

For an affine forward-variance model, the conditional cumulant generating function of the future log return is linear in the entire current forward-variance curve.

Intuition

Read the formula as: “the future-return distribution is built from a weighted combination of today's expected future variances.” The forward curve \(\xi_t(s)\) supplies the variance information; \(g(T-s;z)\) supplies the weights telling us how each horizon contributes to the transform.

The left side is not a probability. It is a compact encoding of the whole distribution of the future log return. This is why it is more useful than merely estimating \(E_t[S_T]\).

Mathematics

\[ \log E_t\!\left[e^{z(X_T-X_t)}\right] =\int_t^T g(T-s;z)\,\xi_t(s)\,ds, \qquad X_t=\log S_t. \]
Read the formula piece by piece
  1. \(X_T-X_t=\log(S_T/S_t)\) is the future log return, not the dollar move \(S_T-S_t\).
  2. \(e^{z(X_T-X_t)}=(S_T/S_t)^z\). It equals \(S_T/S_t\) only when \(z=1\).
  3. Varying \(z\) probes different aspects of the return distribution; for characteristic functions we later use an imaginary transform argument.
  4. \(\xi_t(s)=E_t[V_s]\) is today's expected variance at future time \(s\).
  5. \(g(T-s;z)\) is the unknown deterministic weighting function.
  6. The integral continuously adds the weighted contributions of all future variance horizons between \(t\) and \(T\).

Trading / modeling consequence

If we can determine \(g\), then today's forward-variance curve determines the conditional transform, which in turn determines the risk-neutral terminal distribution used for option pricing.

Common confusionThe goal is not one forecast \(S_T\); it is the distribution of possible \(S_T\)'s. Also, \(z\) is a transform argument, not the future-time variable \(u\) in \(\xi_t(u)\).
Memory ruleCGF = weighted forward-variance curve; \(g\) is the missing set of weights.
13Affine theoremWhich forward-variance models are affine — and why \(\sqrt V\) mattersp. 6

Observation

The lecture characterizes affine forward-variance models by a deterministic lag kernel multiplying \(\sqrt{V_t}\).

Intuition

The \(\sqrt V\) form is what makes the algebra “close.” When Itô squares the diffusion term, \((\sqrt V)^2=V\). Therefore every quadratic-variation contribution is linear in \(V_t\), allowing one common factor \(V_t\) to be pulled out of the drift condition.

If the diffusion depended on a more complicated power of \(V\), Itô would generally create incompatible powers of \(V\), and the clean affine transform equation would not close in the same way.

Mathematics

\[ d\xi_t(u)=\kappa(u-t)\sqrt{V_t}\,dW_t. \]

Quadratic variation gives \(d\langle \xi(u)\rangle_t\propto V_t\,dt\), exactly linear in the state.

Trading / modeling consequence

Classical Heston and rough Heston become two kernel choices inside one affine class: exponential versus power-law/Mittag-Leffler memory.

Common confusionThe theorem constrains the volatility of forward variance; it does not require a flat initial forward curve.
Memory rule\(\sqrt V\) diffusion → \(V\) quadratic variation → affine closure.
14Transform martingaleWhy we introduce \(M_t\), and why \(M_t=e^{zX_t+G_t}\)pp. 6–7

Observation

To determine the unknown weighting function \(g\), the lecture packages the terminal transform into the process \(M_t=E_t[e^{zX_T}]\).

Intuition

This is the key trick. We already know one thing about \(M_t\) before doing any algebra: because it is the conditional expectation of one fixed terminal random variable \(e^{zX_T}\), it must be a martingale. That gives us a restriction strong enough to determine \(g\).

Think of \(M_t\) as the market continually updating its estimate of the same terminal transformed payoff. New information can move that estimate up or down, but the move cannot have a predictable drift under the pricing measure.

Why the exponential form appears

\[ M_t=E_t[e^{zX_T}]. \]
Algebra + affine assumption
  1. Write \(X_T=X_t+(X_T-X_t)\).
  2. Then \(e^{zX_T}=e^{zX_t}e^{z(X_T-X_t)}\).
  3. At time \(t\), \(X_t\) is known, so pull \(e^{zX_t}\) outside the conditional expectation: \[ M_t=e^{zX_t}E_t[e^{z(X_T-X_t)}]. \]
  4. Define the affine CGF \[ G_t=\log E_t[e^{z(X_T-X_t)}] =\int_t^T\xi_t(s)g(T-s;z)ds. \]
  5. Therefore \(E_t[e^{z(X_T-X_t)}]=e^{G_t}\).
  6. Hence \[ M_t=e^{zX_t}e^{G_t}=e^{zX_t+G_t}. \]
Why it is a martingale
  1. For \(s
  2. By the tower property this equals \(E_s[e^{zX_T}]=M_s\).
  3. Therefore the predictable drift of \(M_t\) must be zero.

Trading / modeling consequence

The zero-drift condition is how we convert the stochastic model into a deterministic equation for the unknown transform loading \(g\).

Common confusionWe do not introduce \(M_t\) because we want another random variable. We introduce it because “conditional expectation ⇒ martingale” gives us the equation we need.
Memory ruleIntroduce \(M\) so martingale zero-drift can solve for \(g\).
15Riccati derivationStep 1: where \(G_t\), \(g\), and \(h=\kappa\star g\) come frompp. 6–7

Observation

To apply Itô to \(M_t=e^{zX_t+G_t}\), we first need the dynamics of log spot \(X_t\) and of the forward-variance integral \(G_t\).

Intuition

\(g\) is the unknown set of weights in the affine CGF. When the forward-variance curve itself moves, its shock passes through the memory kernel \(\kappa\) and then through those weights \(g\). The combined effect naturally appears as the convolution \(\kappa\star g\). We often call that combined object \(h\) simply to avoid rewriting the long integral over and over.

Mathematics

\[ dX_t=-\frac12V_tdt+\sqrt{V_t}\,dZ_t, \]
\[ G_t=\int_t^T \xi_t(s)g(T-s;z)\,ds. \]
Differentiate \(G_t\)
  1. The lower limit moves, giving \[ -\xi_t(t)g(T-t;z)dt=-V_tg(\tau;z)dt, \qquad \tau=T-t. \]
  2. The curve itself moves according to \(d\xi_t(s)=\kappa(s-t)\sqrt{V_t}dW_t\).
  3. Insert that shock into the integral: \[ \sqrt{V_t}dW_t\int_t^T\kappa(s-t)g(T-s;z)ds. \]
  4. Change variables to recognize a convolution: \[ (\kappa\star g)(\tau;z) =\int_0^\tau\kappa(\tau-r)g(r;z)dr. \]
  5. Thus \[ dG_t=-V_tg(\tau;z)dt+(\kappa\star g)(\tau;z)\sqrt{V_t}dW_t. \]

Trading / modeling consequence

The model's memory enters the transform calculation only through the convolution \(\kappa\star g\). This is why the same derivation works for exponential and rough kernels.

Common confusion\(h\) is not a new economic factor. It is shorthand for the kernel-adjusted transform loading. Depending on convention, authors may define \(h=\kappa\star g\) or \(h=(\kappa\star g)/\nu\).
Memory rule\(g\) = raw transform weights; \(h\) = those weights after memory kernel \(\kappa\) acts on them.
16Riccati derivationStep 2: why the Riccati equation appears and why we need itp. 7

Observation

The Riccati equation is not an extra modeling assumption. It is the consistency condition that makes the affine transform compatible with the stochastic dynamics.

Intuition

We guessed that the future-return transform can be written using an unknown weight \(g\). Now we must find the one \(g\) that actually matches Heston/rough-Heston dynamics. The martingale condition does exactly that.

Why “Riccati”? Itô's lemma squares diffusion terms. Since the \(G\)-diffusion contains \(h=\kappa\star g\), the quadratic variation creates an \(h^2\) term. A differential/integral equation with constant + linear + quadratic terms is a Riccati equation.

Mathematics

\[ d\langle X\rangle_t=V_tdt,\qquad d\langle G\rangle_t=V_t h^2dt,\qquad d\langle X,G\rangle_t=\rho V_t h\,dt, \quad h=(\kappa\star g)(\tau;z). \]
Apply Itô and force zero drift
  1. Write \(M_t=e^{zX_t+G_t}\).
  2. Itô gives \[ \frac{dM_t}{M_t}=z\,dX_t+dG_t+\frac12z^2d\langle X\rangle_t+\frac12d\langle G\rangle_t+z\,d\langle X,G\rangle_t. \]
  3. Insert the \(X\) and \(G\) dynamics.
  4. Every drift term contains the common factor \(V_tdt\).
  5. The remaining drift coefficient is \[ -\frac z2-g+\frac{z^2}{2}+\rho zh+\frac12h^2. \]
  6. Because \(M_t\) is a martingale, set that drift coefficient equal to zero.
  7. Rearrange: \[ \boxed{ g(\tau;z)=\frac12(z^2-z)+\rho z\,(\kappa\star g)(\tau;z) +\frac12(\kappa\star g)(\tau;z)^2.} \]

Why we care

Solving this equation gives \(g\). Once \(g\) is known, the affine formula gives the characteristic function of the future log-return. Fourier inversion then gives the terminal risk-neutral distribution / option prices. So the practical chain is:

\[ \boxed{\text{Riccati solution }g \longrightarrow \text{ characteristic function } \longrightarrow \text{ distribution of }S_T \longrightarrow \text{ option price}.} \]
Common confusionThe Riccati equation is not trying to predict one \(S_T\). It determines the transform of the entire distribution of possible \(S_T\)'s.
Memory ruleMartingale + Itô ⇒ zero drift ⇒ Riccati ⇒ characteristic function ⇒ option price.
17ConvolutionWhat \((\kappa\star g)(\tau)\) and \(h\) mean intuitivelyp. 7

Observation

The Riccati equation never uses \(g\) alone in the volatility-shock terms; it uses the convolution of \(g\) with the memory kernel \(\kappa\).

Intuition

Imagine \(g(s)\) says how important variance at horizon \(s\) is for the return transform. The kernel \(\kappa(\tau-s)\) says how strongly today's variance shock reaches that horizon. Their convolution combines these two ideas across every intermediate lag.

Calling the result \(h\) is only bookkeeping: \(h\) is the memory-adjusted version of \(g\).

Mathematics

\[ (\kappa\star g)(\tau) =\int_0^\tau\kappa(\tau-s)g(s)\,ds. \]
How to read the integral
  1. Fix total horizon \(\tau\).
  2. Choose an intermediate lag \(s\).
  3. Read \(g(s)\): how much that horizon matters to the transform.
  4. Read \(\kappa(\tau-s)\): how much the volatility shock survives across the remaining lag.
  5. Multiply them.
  6. Add those products over all \(s\in[0,\tau]\).

Trading / modeling consequence

Convolution notation lets one master derivation cover many volatility-memory specifications. Change \(\kappa\), and the same affine machinery still applies.

Common confusionConvolution is not ordinary multiplication. Also, if a later section defines \(h=(\kappa\star g)/\nu\), that is just a rescaling chosen to simplify the rough-Heston equation.
Memory ruleConvolution = combine transform importance with volatility memory across all lags.
18Fractional calculusWhy Gamma and fractional integration appear in rough volatilityp. 8

Observation

Mathematics

19Fractional calculusWhy repeated integration collapses to one power-law kernelp. 8

Observation

Cauchy's repeated-integration formula shows that doing \(n\) nested integrals is equivalent to doing one integral with a polynomial weight.

Intuition

If a value \(f(r)\) enters early, it gets carried through many later layers of integration, so it receives a larger weight. The factor \((t-r)^{n-1}\) measures how much “room” is left for those later integrations. The factorial normalizes the repeated counting.

Mathematics

\[ I^n f(t)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_0^t(t-s)^{n-1}f(s)\,ds. \]
Worked induction step
  1. Assume the formula holds for \(n\).
  2. Write \(I^{n+1}f(t)=\int_0^t I^n f(s)ds\).
  3. Insert \(I^n f(s)=\frac1{(n-1)!}\int_0^s(s-r)^{n-1}f(r)dr\).
  4. Swap integration order over \(0\le r\le s\le t\).
  5. The inner integral becomes \[ \int_r^t(s-r)^{n-1}ds=\frac{(t-r)^n}{n}. \]
  6. Combine \(n(n-1)!=n!\).
  7. Hence \[ I^{n+1}f(t)=\frac1{n!}\int_0^t(t-r)^nf(r)dr. \]
  8. Now replace integer \(n\) by fractional \(\alpha\) and factorial by Gamma to motivate \(I^\alpha\).

Trading / modeling consequence

This is why the rough-Heston power kernel looks exactly like a fractional integration operator.

Common confusionThe power-law weight is not inserted arbitrarily; it is the natural kernel already generated by repeated integration.
Memory ruleNested integrals → one weighted integral → Gamma lets the order become fractional.
20Rough RiccatiThe \(\lambda=0\) fractional Riccati equation — and what \(h\) is doingpp. 8–9

Observation

With a pure power-law kernel, the convolution in the affine Riccati equation is exactly a fractional integral. We therefore introduce \(h\) to package the memory term and then invert that fractional integral.

Intuition

The generic Riccati equation contains the long object \((\kappa\star g)\) repeatedly. In rough Heston, \(\kappa\star g\) is simply \(\nu\) times a fractional integral of \(g\). Defining \(h\) removes that clutter and turns an integral equation with memory into a fractional differential equation.

So \(h\) is not a new market quantity we are trying to estimate. It is a mathematical helper: the transform weight \(g\) after the rough-memory kernel has acted on it.

Professor-style scaled convention

\[ \kappa_0(\tau)=\frac{\nu}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\tau^{\alpha-1}, \qquad \frac{1}{\nu}(\kappa_0\star g)=I^\alpha g. \]

Define

\[ h:=\frac{1}{\nu}(\kappa_0\star g)=I^\alpha g. \]
Worked steps
  1. Start from the generic affine Riccati equation \[ g=\frac12z(z-1)+\rho z(\kappa_0\star g)+\frac12(\kappa_0\star g)^2. \]
  2. Because \((\kappa_0\star g)=\nu h\), substitute \(\nu h\) everywhere: \[ g=\frac12z(z-1)+\rho\nu z h+\frac12\nu^2h^2. \]
  3. Since \(h=I^\alpha g\), apply the fractional derivative \(D^\alpha\).
  4. Using \(D^\alpha I^\alpha g=g\) under the relevant initial conditions, \[ D^\alpha h=g. \]
  5. Substitute for \(g\): \[ \boxed{ D^\alpha h =\frac12z(z-1)+\rho\nu z h+\frac12\nu^2h^2.} \]

Trading / modeling consequence

This is the rough analogue of the ordinary Heston Riccati ODE. The algebraic constant/linear/quadratic structure survives; ordinary differentiation is replaced by a fractional derivative because the variance process has power-law memory.

Common confusionSome references instead define \(h=\kappa\star g\), in which case factors of \(\nu\) move to different places. Check the definition before comparing formulas.
Memory ruleRough memory changes \(d/dt\) into \(D^\alpha\); the Riccati logic itself stays the same.

25Option surfaceRecovering the risk-neutral density from callspp. 26–27 + worked derivation

Observation

An arbitrage-free European call-price curve is decreasing and convex in strike. Its first strike derivative contains a tail probability, while its second strike derivative recovers the risk-neutral density.

Intuition

Raising the strike removes states in which the call pays. The first derivative therefore measures the probability mass above the strike. Differentiating that tail probability once more isolates the density exactly at the strike.

Mathematics

\[C(K,T)=e^{-rT}E^Q[(S_T-K)^+]=e^{-rT}\int_K^\infty (s-K)f_{S_T}(s)\,ds.\]
Worked derivation
  1. Start from risk-neutral pricing. Here (K) is strike, (T) maturity, (r) the continuously compounded risk-free rate, and (f_{S_T}) the risk-neutral density of terminal spot.
  2. Differentiate once with respect to strike. By Leibniz' rule, the lower-limit term is (-(K-K)f_{S_T}(K)=0).
  3. The derivative of the integrand is (\partial_K[(s-K)f_{S_T}(s)]=-f_{S_T}(s)).
  4. Therefore \[C_K(K,T)=-e^{-rT}\int_K^\infty f_{S_T}(s)\,ds=-e^{-rT}Q(S_T>K).\]
  5. Differentiate again. Since (\frac{d}{dK}\int_K^\infty f(s)ds=-f(K)), \[C_{KK}(K,T)=e^{-rT}f_{S_T}(K).\]
  6. At zero rates this reduces to (C_{KK}=f_{S_T}(K)).

Trading / modeling consequence

Because a probability density cannot be negative, no-arbitrage requires (C_{KK}\ge 0): call prices must be convex in strike. A narrow butterfly has price (C(K-h)-2C(K)+C(K+h)\approx h^2C_{KK}(K)), so negative curvature would imply a negative-priced position with a nonnegative terminal payoff.

24Pricing PDEDeriving the Black-Scholes-Merton PDE under Qp. 24

Observation

With deterministic variance, a European option price is a function C(t,Sₜ).

Intuition

Under the pricing measure and zero rates, the option price is a martingale, so its predictable drift must vanish.

Mathematics

Worked steps
  1. Apply Itô's lemma to C(t,Sₜ).
  2. Under Q with zero rates, dSₜ = Sₜ√Vₜ dZₜ, so Eₜ[dSₜ]=0.
  3. The quadratic variation is d⟨S⟩ₜ = VₜSₜ²dt.
  4. Take the conditional expectation of dC.
  5. Only the dt terms remain in the conditional mean.
  6. Set their coefficient equal to zero because C is a Q-martingale.
  7. This yields Cₜ + ½VₜS²Cₛₛ = 0.

Worked steps

  3. Set total gamma to zero: ΓP + nH·ΓH = 0.
  4. Solve nH = −ΓP/ΓH.
  5. Adding that hedge option changes portfolio delta to ΔP + nH·ΔH.
  6. Now use stock, whose gamma is zero and delta is one per share.

02Conditional expectationWhat ξₜ(u)=Eₜ[Vᵤ] meanspp. 1–2

Observation

03Forward varianceDeriving the Heston forward-variance ODEp. 2

Observation

Taking a conditional expectation of the Heston variance SDE removes the future Brownian shock and leaves a deterministic ODE in the future-maturity variable u.

Intuition

The Brownian increment has conditional mean zero. Mean reversion survives because it is the predictable part of future variance dynamics.

Mathematics

Heston: dVᵤ = −λ(Vᵤ−V̄)du + η√Vᵤ dWᵤ. Therefore ∂ᵤξₜ(u) = −λ[ξₜ(u)−V̄].
Worked steps
  1. Fix today's information time t and let u move into the future.
  2. Take Eₜ[·] on both sides of the variance SDE.
  3. Use Eₜ[Vᵤ]=ξₜ(u).
  4. The stochastic increment satisfies Eₜ[√Vᵤ dWᵤ]=0.
  5. What remains is dξₜ(u)=−λ[ξₜ(u)−V̄]du.
  6. Divide by du to get ∂ᵤξₜ(u)=−λ[ξₜ(u)−V̄].

04ODE solutionSolving the forward-variance ODE step by stepp. 2

Observation

The Heston forward variance curve decays exponentially from current variance toward the long-run mean.

Intuition

Subtract the equilibrium V̄ first. The deviation from equilibrium obeys the simplest exponential-decay ODE.

Mathematics

ξₜ(u) = V̄ + (Vₜ−V̄)e^(−λ(u−t)).
Worked steps
  1. Start from ∂ᵤξₜ(u)=−λ[ξₜ(u)−V̄].
  2. Define y(u)=ξₜ(u)−V̄.
  3. Then y′(u)=−λy(u).
  4. Separate variables: dy/y = −λ du.
  5. Integrate from t to u: log y(u) − log y(t) = −λ(u−t).
  6. Exponentiate: y(u)=y(t)e^(−λ(u−t)).
  7. Use ξₜ(t)=Vₜ, hence y(t)=Vₜ−V̄.
  8. Substitute back: ξₜ(u)=V̄+(Vₜ−V̄)e^(−λ(u−t)).

Common confusionDo not memorize the final exponential without noticing the boundary condition at u=t.
Memory ruleSubtract mean → solve exponential decay → add mean back.
05MartingaleWhy ξₜ(u) is a martingale in tp. 2

Laplace{f⋆g}(p)=f̂(p)ĝ(p); κ̂₀(p)=ν/p^α; κ̂(p)=ν/(p^α+λ).
Worked steps
  1. Take the Laplace transform of the convolution equation.
  2. Replace each convolution by a product.
  3. Solve the resulting algebraic equation for κ̂(p).
  4. Invert the transform to recover the time-domain Mittag-Leffler kernel.

Trading / modeling consequence

This is the cleanest route to the mean-reverting rough kernel and its resolvent identity.

Common confusionThe Laplace variable p is a transform variable for lag, not a stock price.
Memory ruleConvolution in time = multiplication in Laplace space.
23ResolventDeriving the resolvent identityp. 10

Observation

The Mittag-Leffler kernel is the resolvent of the pure power-law kernel once mean reversion is introduced.

Intuition

The resolvent is the feedback-adjusted memory kernel after repeatedly accounting for the drift interaction.

Mathematics

κ₀ − κ = λ′(κ₀⋆κ), where λ′=λ/ν.
Worked steps
  1. Start in Laplace space with κ̂ = κ̂₀ / [1 + λ′κ̂₀].
  2. Multiply both sides by 1 + λ′κ̂₀.
  3. Obtain κ̂ + λ′κ̂₀κ̂ = κ̂₀.
  4. Rearrange: κ̂₀ − κ̂ = λ′κ̂₀κ̂.
  5. Apply the inverse Laplace transform.
  6. The product κ̂₀κ̂ becomes the convolution κ₀⋆κ.
  7. This yields κ₀−κ = λ′κ₀⋆κ.

Trading / modeling consequence

The identity lets the mean-reverting rough-Heston model retain the affine forward-variance form.

Common confusionA resolvent here is an exact kernel identity, not merely a numerical approximation.
Memory ruleFeedback-adjusted kernel = resolvent.
24Rough HestonForward variance with mean reversionpp. 10–11

Observation

After mean reversion is resolved, the forward-variance shock still has affine kernel form, now using the Mittag-Leffler kernel.

Intuition

Mean reversion changes the memory shape without destroying affine tractability.

Mathematics

dξₜ(u)=κ(u−t)√VₜdWₜ, with κ(τ)=ντ^(α−1)E(α,α)(−λτ^α).

Trading / modeling consequence

This proves that rough Heston with λ>0 remains inside the affine forward-variance family.

Common confusionBounded-variation drift terms disappear from the martingale dynamics of ξ; they do not disappear from the original variance equation.
Memory ruleMean reversion reshapes κ; affine form survives.
25Classical limitRecovering classical Heston when α=1pp. 9, 12

Observation

The fractional model collapses to classical Heston at H=1/2, so α=1.

Intuition

At α=1, the fractional derivative becomes the ordinary first derivative and the Mittag-Leffler kernel becomes exponential.

Mathematics

α=1 ⇒ H=1/2; E(1,1)(−λτ)=e^(−λτ); D^α becomes d/dτ.

Trading / modeling consequence

This limiting case is an important consistency check on the rough formulas.

26Classical RiccatiFactoring the Heston Riccati ODEp. 12

Observation

In classical Heston, the convolution equation becomes an ordinary quadratic Riccati ODE.

Intuition

A quadratic polynomial can be expressed through its two roots, which makes the ODE separable.

Mathematics

∂τh = ½η²(h−r+)(h−r−), with h(0)=0.
Worked steps
  1. Write the quadratic Riccati right-hand side in standard polynomial form.
  2. Solve the quadratic equation to obtain roots r+ and r−.
  3. Factor the polynomial as ½η²(h−r+)(h−r−).
  4. Now the variables h and τ can be separated.

Trading / modeling consequence

The root representation is the starting point for the closed-form Heston transform.

Common confusionThe roots depend on the transform argument as well as model parameters; they are not fixed constants across Fourier frequencies.
Memory ruleQuadratic Riccati → factor by roots.
27Classical RiccatiSolving the Heston Riccati ODE line by linep. 12

Observation

The factored Heston Riccati equation has a rational-exponential closed form.

Intuition

Partial fractions convert the nonlinear ODE into a difference of logarithms.

Mathematics

h(τ) = r−[1−e^(−dτ)] / [1−(r−/r+)e^(−dτ)], with d = ½η²(r+−r−).
Worked steps
  1. Separate variables: dh/[(h−r+)(h−r−)] = ½η² dτ.
  2. Use partial fractions: 1/[(h−r+)(h−r−)] = [1/(r+−r−)]{1/(h−r+) − 1/(h−r−)}.
  3. Integrate both sides.
  4. The left side becomes a logarithm of the ratio (h−r+)/(h−r−).
  5. Exponentiate the resulting equation.
  6. Use h(0)=0 to determine the integration constant.
  7. Define d=½η²(r+−r−) and solve algebraically for h.
  8. The result is the displayed rational-exponential formula.

Trading / modeling consequence

This closed form makes classical Heston option pricing computationally efficient.

Common confusionDo not memorize the final fraction before understanding the factorization, partial fractions, and boundary condition.
Memory ruleFactor → partial fractions → logs → boundary condition.
28Characteristic functionAssembling the Heston C and D functionspp. 12–13

Observation

The affine transform separates into a loading on current variance and a loading on long-run variance.

Intuition

Because the forward-variance curve is exponential, integrating g against it splits naturally into current-state and long-run-mean pieces.

Mathematics

φₜ(T;a)=Eₜ[e^(iaX_T)] = exp{iaXₜ + C(a,τ)V̄ + D(a,τ)Vₜ}.
Worked steps
  1. Start from the affine forward-variance transform integral.
  2. Insert ξₜ(s)=V̄+(Vₜ−V̄)e^(−λ(s−t)).
  3. The exponential-weighted integral is the h/D term.
  4. The remaining long-run-mean contribution integrates into C.
  5. Collect coefficients multiplying Vₜ and V̄.

Trading / modeling consequence

The C-D representation is the standard computational form used in Heston Fourier pricing.

Common confusionC and D are deterministic transform functions, not additional stochastic factors.
Memory ruleHeston CF = exp(i a X + C V̄ + D V).
29Information equivalenceFrom characteristic function to density and option pricespp. 13–14

Observation

At one expiry, European option prices, the risk-neutral density, implied volatilities, and the characteristic function encode the same terminal distributional information under regularity conditions.

Intuition

They are different mathematical representations of the same risk-neutral law of the terminal underlying.

Mathematics

φ(a)=E[e^(iaX_T)]; density p(y)=1/(2π)∫ e^(−iay)φ(a) da.
Worked steps
  1. Use Fourier inversion to recover the density from φ.
  2. Integrate option payoffs against that density to obtain European option prices.
  3. Invert Black-Scholes price at each strike to obtain implied volatility.
  4. Conversely, a smooth complete option-price surface gives density by strike differentiation and therefore its characteristic function by Fourier transform.

Trading / modeling consequence

Transform methods can price a whole strike grid efficiently and are central to calibration.

Common confusionEquivalent information does not mean equivalent numerical stability; each representation has different interpolation and estimation issues.
Memory ruleSurface ↔ density ↔ characteristic function.
30Empirical diagnosticsVariance memory and rough versus classical Hestonpp. 32–45

Observation

The affine kernel yields explicit variance covariance formulas. Classical Heston implies exponential memory; rough Heston produces scale-rich dependence and fits the empirical variance ACF better in the lecture experiment.

Intuition

Two future variances co-move because both inherit overlapping exposures to the same earlier variance shocks. The kernel controls that overlap.

Mathematics

Covₜ(V_T,V_(T+Δ)) = ∫ₜᵀ ξₜ(s)κ(T−s)κ(T+Δ−s)ds. Classical Heston gives roughly exp(−λΔ); rough Heston gives Varₜ(V_T) proportional to τ^(2H) at short scales.
Worked steps
  1. For classical Heston, insert κ(x)=ηe^(−λx).
  2. The product of the two kernels contributes e^(−λΔ)e^(−2λ(T−s)).
  3. After normalization by Var(V_T), the Δ-dependence is e^(−λΔ).
  4. For rough Heston with λ=0 and flat forward variance, insert κ(x) proportional to x^(α−1).
  5. Variance integrates κ² over the horizon: ∫₀^τ u^(2α−2)du.
  6. Because α=H+1/2, the exponent after integration is 2α−1=2H.
  7. Thus Var(V_T) scales like τ^(2H), matching the rough scaling studied in Lecture 2.

Trading / modeling consequence

The lecture's rough-Heston ACF fit gives H around 0.16, close to the earlier roughness estimate, while smile experiments show that increasing H flattens short-dated smiles.

Common confusionAutocorrelation ρV(Δ) is not the same object as the Brownian spot/variance correlation parameter ρ.
Memory ruleKernel overlap determines memory; H links time-series roughness to option-smile behavior.
