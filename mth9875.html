The Volatility Surface

The first two lectures build one continuous argument: empirical market data reject constant-volatility thinking; stochastic volatility explains changing variance and smiles; rough volatility explains the observed short-time scaling; local volatility shows how a vanilla option surface can be represented by a state-dependent diffusion.

Course backbone

Data → model restriction → option surface

Stylized facts → random variance → stochastic volatility → roughness → P vs Q → Heston → Dupire local volatility → implied-volatility geometry

The guiding rule is not to memorize isolated equations. For each one, ask what empirical fact motivates it, what assumption is being made, and what observable quantity it helps explain.

How to use this site

Four learning layers

  • Observation: what the data or option market shows.
  • Intuition: why the phenomenon occurs.
  • Mathematics: the formula and derivation.
  • Modeling consequence: what a valid volatility model must reproduce.

Lecture 1 · Stylized facts

The lecture begins from data. The goal is to identify empirical regularities that any useful volatility model must respect.

01Power lawsTail probability versus densityCore concept

Observation

Many financial and economic quantities have tails that decay much more slowly than a Gaussian tail.

Intuition

A power law has no single characteristic scale in the tail. Very large observations remain materially more likely than under an exponential or Gaussian decay.

Mathematics

Tail law\[P(X>x)\sim Cx^{-\zeta}.\]

If the tail probability behaves like (x^{-\zeta}), then the density behaves like (x^{-(\zeta+1)}).

Worked derivation
  1. Start from (P(X>x)\approx Cx^{-\zeta}).
  2. The density is (f(x)=-\frac{d}{dx}P(X>x)).
  3. Differentiate: (-\frac{d}{dx}(Cx^{-\zeta})=C\zeta x^{-(\zeta+1)}).
  4. So the tail exponent is (zeta), while the density exponent is (zeta+1).

Modeling consequence

Always check what is plotted. A log-log slope near (-3) for a tail probability corresponds to roughly (-4) for the density.

RememberTail exponent and density exponent differ by one.
Common trapReading a histogram slope as if it were a survival-function slope.
02Power-law algebraHow exponents transformWorked rules

Intuition

The fattest tail dominates sums and maxima. Transformations such as (Y=X^\alpha) rescale the exponent.

Mathematics

\[\zeta_{X_1+\cdots+X_n}=\min_i\zeta_i,\qquad \zeta_{\max(X_i)}=\min_i\zeta_i.\]
\[Y=X^\alpha\quad\Rightarrow\quad \zeta_Y=\frac{\zeta_X}{\alpha}.\]
Worked derivation of (Y=X^\alpha)
  1. (P(Y>y)=P(X^\alpha>y)).
  2. For (alpha>0), this is (P(X>y^{1/\alpha})).
  3. Use the tail law: (P(X>x)\sim x^{-\zeta_X}).
  4. Then (P(Y>y)\sim (y^{1/\alpha})^{-\zeta_X}=y^{-\zeta_X/\alpha}).
  5. Therefore (zeta_Y=\zeta_X/\alpha).

Modeling consequence

This algebra lets one connect power laws across trade size, market impact, firm size, and returns without refitting every relation from scratch.

03Pareto / ZipfWhy 80/20 implies an exponent near oneWorked calculation

Observation

Income, wealth, city size, and firm size often display extreme concentration.

Mathematics

\[W=P^{1-1/\alpha}.\]
Worked 80/20 calculation
  1. Set (P=0.2) and (W=0.8).
  2. (0.8=0.2^{1-1/\alpha}).
  3. Take logs: (log 0.8=(1-1/\alpha)log 0.2).
  4. Divide: (1-1/\alpha=\log(0.8)/\log(0.2)\approx0.13865).
  5. So (1/\alpha\approx0.86135).
  6. (alpha\approx1/0.86135\approx1.161).

Modeling consequence

An exponent only modestly above one means an extremely fat tail and severe concentration.

04ReturnsLog returns are not GaussianEmpirical foundation

Observation

SPX daily log returns have a sharper center and much fatter tails than a normal distribution.

Mathematics

\[r_t=\log\frac{S_t}{S_{t-1}}.\]
\[P(|r|>x)\sim x^{-3}\quad\Rightarrow\quad f(r)\sim |r|^{-4}.\]

Intuition

A Student-(t) with about three degrees of freedom mimics this empirical tail behavior much better than a Gaussian.

Modeling consequence

A constant-volatility Gaussian model cannot reproduce the empirical frequency of extreme daily moves.

05Tail truncationWhy a financial power law cannot continue foreverConceptual proof

Intuition

Stock prices are exponentials of log returns. If positive log-return tails stayed power-law forever, exponential moments could blow up.

Mathematics

\[S=e^X,\qquad E[S]=E[e^X].\]

A polynomially decaying positive tail is not enough to control the exponential factor (e^X) at infinity.

Modeling consequence

Interpret empirical “power law” as scaling over a substantial range, not as a literal law from zero to infinity.

06Stylized factsReturns are hard to predict; volatility is persistentCore intuition

Observation

Raw returns have little serial correlation, while (|r_t|) and (r_t^2) exhibit persistent autocorrelation.

Intuition

The sign of tomorrow’s return is difficult to forecast, but the scale of tomorrow’s move is much more predictable.

Modeling consequence

Volatility itself should be a state variable with persistence. This is the empirical motivation for stochastic volatility.

07Business timeIntegrated variance as a random clockBridge to SV

Intuition

A calendar day can contain very different amounts of “financial time.” Quiet days are short in business time; crisis days are long.

Mathematics

\[\tau(t)=\int_0^t v_s\,ds.\]

Conditionally on a path of variance, Brownian returns accumulate variance according to this random clock.

Modeling consequence

Mixtures of Gaussian returns with different realized variances naturally create a high central peak and fat unconditional tails.

08Conditional GaussianityWhy random variance creates fat tailsKey bridge

Intuition

Inside each volatility regime, returns may look close to Gaussian. Mixing low- and high-volatility regimes makes the unconditional distribution sharply peaked with fat tails.

Mathematics

\[\frac{dS_t}{S_t}=\sqrt{V_t}\,dZ_t.\]

Modeling consequence

Instead of forcing one exotic static return distribution, model a random, persistent variance process.

09Cont stylized factsThe checklist a volatility model should respectReview map

Observation

Key facts include weak return autocorrelation, heavy tails, intermittency, volatility clustering, slow decay of volatility dependence, leverage, conditional heavy tails, and volume-volatility correlation.

Modeling consequence

A model that fits one option smile but destroys these dynamics is descriptively incomplete.

10Leverage effectWhy price declines and volatility rises are linkedSurface connection

Observation

Equity-index price moves and volatility changes are strongly negatively related.

Mathematics

\[\operatorname{Corr}(d\log S_t,d\log V_t)<0.\]

Modeling consequence

Negative spot-vol correlation is a primary source of negative equity implied-volatility skew in stochastic-volatility models.

11Rough volatilityThe Hurst exponent and scalingCore concept

Observation

Log realized variance increments display power-law scaling across time horizons.

Mathematics

\[E\left[|\log V_{t+\Delta}-\log V_t|^q\right]\propto \Delta^{qH}.\]

Intuition

(H=1/2) is Brownian roughness. Empirical estimates well below (1/2) imply even more jagged volatility paths.

How (H) is estimated
  1. Choose moments (q).
  2. For each lag (Delta), compute the empirical (q)-th absolute moment of log-vol increments.
  3. Take logs: (log m_q(\Delta)=a_q+\zeta_q\log\Delta).
  4. The slope gives (zeta_q).
  5. Under monofractal scaling, (zeta_q=qH), so regress (zeta_q) on (q) through the origin to estimate (H).

Modeling consequence

Conventional smooth Markovian volatility diffusions miss this short-scale roughness.

12Volatility surfaceFrom flat Black–Scholes vol to market smilesOption data

Observation

Equity implied volatility depends on strike and maturity. Lower strikes usually trade at higher implied volatility.

Mathematics

\[k=\log(K/F),\qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{imp}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{imp}}(k,T).\]

Modeling consequence

A useful model must fit not only one volatility level but the cross-sectional shape and its dynamics.

13Short-dated skewPower-law explosion of ATM skewEmpirical restriction

Mathematics

\[\text{ATM Skew}(T)=\left.\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{imp}}(k,T)}{\partial k}\right|_{k=0},\qquad |\text{Skew}(T)|\sim T^{-\gamma}.\]

Intuition

Short expiries react most violently to near-term downside risk, so the skew steepens as maturity shrinks.

Modeling consequence

The observed short-time exponent is a direct restriction on admissible volatility dynamics and is one of the main empirical arguments for rough volatility.

14Surface dynamicsSkew-stickiness ratioDynamics test

Observation

ATM implied volatility co-moves with spot in a fairly stable way across expiries on a given day.

Mathematics

\[R(T)=\frac{\beta(T)}{\psi(T)}.\]

Here (psi(T)) is ATM strike skew and (eta(T)) is the spot/ATM-vol regression coefficient in quadratic-covariation form.

Intuition

Sticky delta gives (R=0); sticky strike gives (R=1). Empirical values are neither extreme and are roughly expiry-independent on a given day.

Modeling consequence

Matching a static smile is insufficient: the model must also reproduce how that smile moves when spot moves.

15Stochastic volatilityWhy go beyond Black–Scholes?Motivation

Observation

Volatility fluctuates through time and vanilla option prices imply smiles, not a flat volatility.

Intuition

Black–Scholes can still be a quoting formula, but its single-volatility world is not a realistic joint model for spot and option dynamics.

Mathematics

\[\frac{dS_t}{S_t}=\sqrt{V_t}\,dZ_t,\qquad V_t=\frac{d}{dt}\langle\log S\rangle_t.\]

Modeling consequence

SV models can generate realistic smiles while preserving arbitrage-free dynamics, making them more coherent for exotic-option pricing and hedging.

16Mixture intuitionWhy random volatility creates fat tailsToy model

Intuition

Low-volatility regimes create the sharp center; high-volatility regimes create the fat tails. Mixing them reproduces the stylized shape of return histograms.

Mathematics

\[f(x)=\tfrac12\phi(x;0,0.1^2)+\tfrac12\phi(x;0,0.3^2).\]

Modeling consequence

Variance should be random and persistent, not a one-off jump variable.

17Variance dynamicsWhy variance should be stationary in the long runEconomic argument

Intuition

If volatility had no long-run restoring force, the distribution of a large company’s volatility decades ahead would drift to absurdly tiny or huge values. Mean reversion keeps long-run variance economically plausible.

Modeling consequence

Classical models such as Heston build stationarity through mean-reverting variance dynamics.

18Fractional Brownian motionHow (H) changes dependence and roughnessRough-vol basis

Mathematics

\[E[W_t^H W_s^H]=\tfrac12\big(|t|^{2H}+|s|^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\big).\]

Intuition

(H=1/2) gives Brownian motion; (H>1/2) gives positively correlated increments; (H<1/2) gives negatively correlated increments and rougher paths.

Modeling consequence

Rough-vol models use fractional-type behavior for volatility, not for the tradable stock price itself.

19Measures(P) versus (Q): forecasting versus pricingEssential distinction

Intuition

(P) describes real-world statistical evolution; (Q) is the pricing measure under which discounted tradable asset prices are martingales.

Mathematics

Under (P)\[\frac{dS_t}{S_t}=\mu_t\,dt+\sqrt{V_t}\,dZ_t^P.\]
Under (Q), with zero rates\[\frac{dS_t}{S_t}=\sqrt{V_t}\,dZ_t^Q.\]

Modeling consequence

Historical volatility forecasting is a (P)-problem; vanilla and exotic option pricing is primarily a (Q)-problem.

20Risk premiumWhy the equity risk premium can be proportional to varianceWorked derivation

Mathematics

With fraction (pi_t) of wealth in stock,

\[\frac{dW_t}{W_t}=\pi_t\mu_tdt+\pi_t\sqrt{V_t}\,dZ_t^P.\]
Worked log-utility optimization
  1. Apply Itô to (log W_t): [d\log W_t=(\pi_t\mu_t-\tfrac12\pi_t^2V_t)dt+\pi_t\sqrt{V_t}dZ_t^P.]
  2. The instantaneous expected growth rate is (g(\pi)=\pi\mu-\tfrac12\pi^2V).
  3. Differentiate: (g'(\pi)=\mu-\pi V).
  4. Set to zero: (pi^*=\mu/V).
  5. If the optimal share (pi^*=1/\beta) is constant, then (mu=\beta V).

Modeling consequence

This motivates a Girsanov shift proportional to (sqrt V), linking (P)- and (Q)-dynamics.

21Change of measureGirsanov shift from (P) to (Q)Worked substitution

Mathematics

\[dZ_t^Q=dZ_t^P+\beta\sqrt{V_t}\,dt.\]
Why the drift disappears
  1. Start under (P): (dS/S=\beta Vdt+\sqrt V,dZ^P).
  2. Rearrange the measure change: (dZ^P=dZ^Q-\beta\sqrt Vdt).
  3. Substitute: (dS/S=\beta Vdt+\sqrt V(dZ^Q-\beta\sqrt Vdt)).
  4. The drift terms cancel: (eta Vdt-\beta Vdt=0).
  5. Hence (dS/S=\sqrt V,dZ^Q), making (S) a (Q)-martingale when rates are zero.
22Classical HestonMean-reverting square-root varianceCanonical SV model

Mathematics

\[dV_t=-\lambda(V_t-\bar V)dt+\eta\sqrt{V_t}\,dW_t,\qquad d\langle W,Z\rangle_t=\rho,dt.\]

Intuition

(lambda) controls mean-reversion speed, (ar V) the long-run variance, (eta) vol-of-vol, and ( ho) spot-vol correlation. Negative ( ho) creates negative equity skew.

Modeling consequence

Heston is analytically convenient and economically interpretable, but conventional Heston does not reproduce the empirical short-time rough scaling well.

23Heston under (P) and (Q)How the variance parameters changeMeasure effect

Mathematics

Under (P)\[dV_t=-\lambda^P(V_t-\bar V^P)dt+\eta\sqrt{V_t},dW_t^P.\]
Under (Q)\[dV_t=-\lambda^Q(V_t-\bar V^Q)dt+\eta\sqrt{V_t},dW_t^Q.\]
\[\lambda^Q=\lambda^P+\rho\eta,\qquad \lambda^Q\bar V^Q=\lambda^P\bar V^P.\]

Modeling consequence

Option markets reveal (Q)-parameters. Historical estimation reveals (P)-parameters. Their difference is volatility risk compensation.

24Local volatilityWhat local volatility is—and is notPhilosophy

Intuition

Local volatility is the unique one-factor diffusion that reproduces all vanilla option prices across strikes and maturities. It need not be interpreted as the true physical law of volatility.

Modeling consequence

Think of local volatility as an “effective theory” or state-dependent projection of richer stochastic-volatility dynamics.

25Option surfaceRecovering the risk-neutral density from callsKey identity

Mathematics

\[C(K,T)=E^Q[(S_T-K)^+],\qquad \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial K^2}=\phi(K,T).\]
Derivative logic
  1. (partial_K(S_T-K)^+=-1_{\{S_T>K\}}).
  2. Thus (partial_K C=-Q(S_T>K)).
  3. Differentiate again with respect to (K): (partial_{KK}C=\phi(K,T)), the density at (K).

Modeling consequence

Vanilla option prices across strikes encode the full (Q)-distribution of terminal spot.

26DupireLocal variance = calendar spread / butterflyCore formula

Mathematics

With zero rates and dividends,

\[\sigma_\ell^2(K,T)=\frac{2\,\partial_T C(K,T)}{K^2\,\partial_{KK}C(K,T)}.\]

Intuition

The numerator measures how option value changes with maturity (calendar spread); the denominator measures strike curvature (butterfly / density).

Modeling consequence

A complete smooth vanilla surface uniquely determines the local-volatility function.

27Projection resultLocal variance as conditional expected stochastic varianceDeep intuition

Mathematics

\[\sigma_\ell^2(K,t)=E^Q[V_t\mid S_t=K].\]

Intuition

Among all stochastic-volatility paths that arrive at the same spot (K) at time (t), local variance is their conditional average instantaneous variance.

Modeling consequence

This formalizes the idea that local volatility is an effective one-dimensional projection of a higher-dimensional stochastic-volatility world.

28Implied-vol coordinatesLocal variance in terms of total implied varianceSurface calculus

Mathematics

Define total implied variance (w(k,T)=\Sigma^2(k,T)T). Then Dupire can be rewritten entirely in terms of (w) and its strike/maturity derivatives.

\[v_\ell(k,T)=\frac{\partial_T w}{\left(1-\frac{k}{2w}\partial_k w\right)^2-\frac14\left(\frac14+\frac1w\right)(\partial_k w)^2+\frac12\partial_{kk}w}.\]

Modeling consequence

This is the practical bridge from a parametrized implied-volatility surface to the local-volatility surface used in a PDE or simulation.

29BBF formulaShort-time relation between local vol and implied volWorked interpretation

Mathematics

\[\frac{1}{\Sigma_0(k)}=\frac1k\int_0^k\frac{dz}{\sigma_\ell(z)}.\]

Intuition

Short-maturity implied volatility is approximately a harmonic-type average of local volatility along the path from ATM (0) to log-strike (k).

How to use it
  1. Start with a time-homogeneous local-vol curve (sigma_\ell(k)).
  2. Integrate reciprocal local vol from (0) to (k).
  3. Divide by (k).
  4. Invert to obtain the leading short-maturity implied volatility (Sigma_0(k)).

Modeling consequence

The approximation is conceptually useful, but for accurate practical local-vol pricing, numerical PDE methods are generally preferred.

30Big pictureHow Lectures 1 and 2 connectOne-page memory map

Observation

Returns are heavy-tailed, volatility clusters, spot-vol co-moves negatively, and short-dated skew follows power-law scaling.

Intuition

Random business time explains why changing variance can create heavy tails. Roughness explains why short-time volatility changes are more jagged than ordinary diffusion intuition suggests.

Mathematics

\[\text{Data}\Rightarrow V_t\text{ random}\Rightarrow H<1/2\Rightarrow \text{SV/rough-vol restrictions}.\]

Modeling consequence

(P) describes forecasting, (Q) describes pricing. Heston gives a tractable stochastic-volatility benchmark; Dupire gives the unique local-volatility projection consistent with the vanilla surface.
